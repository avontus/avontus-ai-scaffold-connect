# Estimating scaffold labor from an Avontus Viewer design

Avontus Viewer supplies the quantities. The user supplies the labor units and
productivity factors, from their own Schedule of Norms or recorded actuals.

1. **Get the quantities from the connector.** Read the design through the
   Avontus Viewer tools and use the figures and notes they return. Do not
   estimate or reconstruct a quantity the connector does not provide.

2. **Use only the user's labor units.** They are usually stated as minutes per
   piece. Convert to man-hours and show it: 6 minutes per piece is 0.10
   man-hours per piece.

3. **Never supply a labor unit or factor.** There is no universal scaffold
   labor standard, and a rate that is not the user's own can put a real bid at
   risk. If the user has none, ask for their estimating basis. Do not offer an
   "industry convention" or a typical range.

4. **Ask how their factors combine.** Factors may multiply, add, apply to only
   part of the work, or already be built into the labor unit. Do not assume.

5. **Treat erection, modification and dismantling separately.** Each has its
   own labor unit. Never reuse one activity's unit or factors for another
   without asking.

6. **Show every step.** Name the source of each input, then the arithmetic,
   so the estimate can be checked:
   157 pieces x 6 min = 942 min = 15.7 man-hours, then 15.7 x 1.25 = 19.6.

7. **Man-hours are not duration.** Dividing by crew size gives crew-hours,
   not a schedule.
